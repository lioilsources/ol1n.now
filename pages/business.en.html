Job-site time tracking that doesn’t track people

Your people start and stop the timer on their phones at the building site or at the customer’s. You see hours by person and by job, and you know they were on site. Where they were in between, the app doesn’t know.

What you get

Your ancestors, traced in parish registers

I find them in digitised parish registers and back each one up with the register entry. You get a family tree you can click through all the way to the original.

What you get

  • Your family line written up generation by generation: names, dates, places and occupations, always with a link to the entry in the archive.
  • A private interactive tree in your browser.
  • Transcriptions of entries from Kurrent (old German handwriting) and Latin into readable form.
  • A list of uncertain points: where an entry couldn’t be read or the line branches.

What I need from you

  • The oldest ancestor you know of: name, approximate date and place of birth.
  • Ideally someone born before 1925. Archives don’t publish more recent entries.

Straight talk

  • I search the registers of the State Regional Archives in Prague and the Moravian Land Archives in Brno, which roughly means Central Bohemia and South Moravia. Nowhere else yet.
  • Registers only become public after a certain time: births after 100 years, marriages after 75, deaths after 30. Younger generations you document from family papers.
  • Machine reading of Kurrent isn’t 100% accurate. I check every entry in the main line by hand.
  • Where a register is missing or has burned, the line ends. You pay for the research, not for the number of generations.

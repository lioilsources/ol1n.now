tagline: A non-linear reader for ancient texts — 92 works, Czech summaries and the original
---
Kindlify is a reader for the great old texts that people rarely read from the
first page to the last. You see a work as a **tree of chapters**, and every node
comes with a **Czech summary** at hand: what the chapter is about and what makes
it special. That way you can map out Plato's Republic or the Iliad before you
start reading.

### Original and verses

For the shorter works (53 of them: the Greek New Testament, Plato's dialogues, all
the tragedies and comedies, Cicero's writings, the Tao Te Ching) the tree continues below the chapters down to **text passages** — for the Bible
by verse, "1:1–12", "1:11–23" — and each passage has its own Czech gloss.
One tap switches from the summary to the **original text**: Mark in Greek,
Virgil's Georgics in Latin, Laozi in Classical Chinese. The summary serves as a map
and the original is right next to it.

### Word cloud as a filter

Above the summary is a cloud of terms from that part of the work. The big ones are those
that set this chapter apart from the others, not the ones that are everywhere. You can
**tap to select** terms and the reader then shows which chapters talk about them:
justice in the Republic, water in the Tao Te Ching. The terms include words
in their **original script** (無為, ἀγαθόν), because the library keeps the originals.

### Where the texts come from

The works come from the **WorldLibraryProject** corpus, a multilingual library of
philosophical and sacred texts in their original languages. Chapter summaries,
Czech titles, glosses and keywords are produced by the librarian (an LLM) directly from
the original; no translations of the works are added. As the librarian makes progress,
more works arrive in the app.

### Books in the app (version 1.3)

**Greek philosophy.** Plato: Apology, Crito, Euthyphro, Phaedo,
Meno, Gorgias, Protagoras, Symposium, Phaedrus, Republic, Theaetetus,
Parmenides, Sophist, Timaeus, Laws. Aristotle: Nicomachean Ethics,
Politics, Metaphysics, Poetics, Rhetoric. Xenophon: Memorabilia,
Anabasis. Epictetus: Discourses. Marcus Aurelius: Meditations. Diogenes
Laertius: Lives of the Eminent Philosophers.

**Greek drama and epic.** Homer: Iliad, Odyssey. Aeschylus: Agamemnon, The Libation
Bearers, The Eumenides, The Persians, Prometheus Bound. Sophocles: Oedipus the King,
Oedipus at Colonus, Antigone, Electra. Euripides: Medea, Hippolytus,
The Bacchae, The Trojan Women. Aristophanes: The Clouds, The Birds, The Frogs, Lysistrata.
Apollonius of Rhodes: Argonautica.

**Greek historiography.** Herodotus: Histories. Thucydides: History of the Peloponnesian
War.

**Greek New Testament.** The Gospels of Matthew, Mark, Luke and John, the Acts
of the Apostles, the Letter to the Romans, Revelation — with verses and the Greek text.

**Latin literature.** Virgil: Aeneid, Georgics, Eclogues.
Ovid: Metamorphoses. Horace: Odes. Catullus: Poems. Lucretius: On the Nature of Things.
Cicero: On the Republic, On Duties, On the Nature of the Gods, On Friendship, On Old Age.
Caesar: Commentaries on the Gallic War. Sallust: The Conspiracy of Catiline. Tacitus:
Annals, Germania. Seneca: Letters to Lucilius.

**Chinese classics.** Laozi: Tao Te Ching. Confucius: Analects (Lunyu).

**Pali Canon.** Anguttara Nikaya: the Books of the Ones through the Elevens (all
eleven). Digha Nikaya: the Division on Morality. Khuddaka Nikaya: Buddhavamsa,
Cariyapitaka. Abhidhamma: Points of Controversy, Discourse on Elements, Analyses.

**German philosophy.** Hegel: Phenomenology of Spirit, Science of Logic.

Built with Flutter (Riverpod, Drift). Runs on macOS, iOS and Android,
and all the works are available offline in the app.

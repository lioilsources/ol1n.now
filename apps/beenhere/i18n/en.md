---
tagline: Photos from the place you're standing right now
---
You arrive somewhere you've been before. Been Here shows you the photos you took
there, grouped by visit: last year, five years ago, that one weekend in 2019.

What matters is the moment you arrive, not the gallery. That's why the app can
speak up on its own: it keeps geofences around the places you care about, and
when you come back, you get a notification.

### Everything stays on your phone

No server, no account, no third-party analytics. Neither your photos nor their
coordinates ever leave the phone. The app doesn't copy your photos — it keeps
only an index, meaning ids and metadata; the system library stays the source of
truth.

Only two things can go out, both are off by default and both can be switched in
settings: a coordinate sent to the system geocoder so a place gets a name, and
map tiles from OpenStreetMap while the map is open.

### The places you live shouldn't wake you up

Places are clustered from your photos. A place where you shoot on too many
different days is muted automatically — where you live isn't where you travel
to. You set the search radius around you with a slider.

On top of that, notifications stay quiet within a radius around home: 25 km by
default, adjustable from zero to a hundred in settings. You don't set home: it's
the place with photos from the most different days. Settings then work out why
the app is silent: how many places the system is watching, how far the nearest
one is and when the last notification came.

### Map

The map is off by default. When you turn it on, it shows photos as dots around
where you're standing. Tap a dot to open that photo in the visit it belongs to.
Pan the map to look somewhere else and the screen moves with it. You can change
the radius with a slider or with a gesture on the map.

A second slider with two handles decides how old the dots can be: say the last
14 days, or from two weeks to two years back. The default is the last two years.
It filters only the dots; the visit timeline still shows everything.

### Then and now

With an old photo you can stand in the same spot and take it again. The
resulting pair is drawn onto a canvas, not taken as a screenshot — what's shared
is the two photographs, so the two photographs are what's drawn, both at the same
height and in full resolution.

Targets iOS 15+ and Android 8+.

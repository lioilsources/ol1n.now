Chess Kingdom teaches chess to a child who can't read yet. Every piece is a
character with its own name and personality: Daisy Tower has wheels and only
drives straight, Glimmer Butterfly flies on the slant, the pony jumps in a
hook, and King Rainbeard walks slowly because he has the finest beard in the
kingdom. The child doesn't play a whole game straight away. They travel across
a map of islands and learn one piece on each.

### The kingdom map

The islands follow one another along a winding path: **The Kingdom** (the
board as a town with streets and houses), **Little Bridesmaids** (pawns),
**Rook on Wheels**, **Butterfly** (bishop), **Princess** (queen), **Pony** and
**Daddy King**. Each island has three to six lessons, and each lesson a few
short tasks. The next island opens once the previous one is done. Stars from
the lessons add up on the map, so the child can see how much they've already
mastered.

Seven islands and 31 lessons are finished so far, from "tap the room e4" to
"save the king". Six more (Battles and Apples, Checkmate, Magic Moves,
Openings, Tricks and The Big Game) are locked on the map, waiting to be
written.

### Lessons as a game

A lesson opens with a short story and then sets a task: drive the rook to the
star, collect all the stars, avoid the lava, take all the rival's pawns, tap
every square the pony can jump to, step the king out of check. When the child
taps a piece, the squares it may move to light up. Lessons without a king use
only piece moves, with no check and no castling. The real rules arrive step by
step.

Nobody stops the child. The number of moves only decides whether they get
three stars or two. When things aren't working, the app shows where the piece
may go after the first mistake, and suggests the first move after the third.
Every lesson is checked automatically: a solver walks through all the moves
and confirms the task can be done in the set number of moves.

### Everything is read aloud

The app works without reading. A narrator reads the story, the task, the
"well done" and "let's try again" replies and the characters' lines aloud.
Tapping the speech bubble reads the task again. The voices are pre-recorded
from text (Piper for Czech, Kokoro for English), so they sound the same on
every phone and work offline.

### Battles

When a piece captures, the board disappears for a moment and a duel plays out
full screen. The characters face each other in the rival's arena, swap a few
words ("Choo-choo!" – "Off to the pond…") and one of them goes off to rest.
Nobody dies: the defeated piece heads "back to the depot" or "off to the
meadow". A battle lasts under three seconds and can be skipped with a tap.

### Fairies and witches

There are two armies: the **Fairies** (King Rainbeard, Princess Sparkle, Daisy
Tower, a unicorn instead of the pony) and the **Witches** (King Grumble, who
grumbles but loves cupcakes, Queen Thunder, whose spells go boom but never hurt
anyone, Batty Tower, and frogs in hats instead of pawns). Each has six
characters, its own arena and its own lines.
The child chooses who to play as and against whom. The characters are
original, with no brands or well-known fairy-tale licences. They were made
with the FLUX.1-schnell model (Apache 2.0 licence) and then hand-picked and
cleaned up.

### Playing against the robot

The pawn war at the end of the bridesmaids' island is played against the
computer, against Baby the robot. The robot has four strength levels, from
completely clueless to one that already spots captures. A standalone game
against the robot, or against a parent on one phone, is still to come.

### Status

The app is a work in progress and not available for download yet. Next up are
the remaining islands, a parents' zone (settings, turning battles off, an
overview of what the child can do), tournaments at the end of the islands with
a diploma, and a test with a real five-year-old chess player: can she tell
the fairies apart on a small board?

No ads, no account, offline. In Czech and English.

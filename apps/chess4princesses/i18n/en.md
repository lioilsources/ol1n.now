name: Chess Kingdom
tagline: Chess for five-year-olds — fairy-tale lessons, a voice that reads everything aloud, battles between fourteen armies and famous games of the masters
---
Chess Kingdom teaches chess to a child who can't read yet. Every piece is a
character with its own name and personality: Daisy Tower has wheels and only
drives straight, Glimmer Butterfly flies on the slant, the pony jumps in a
hook, and King Rainbeard walks slowly because he has the finest beard in the
kingdom. The child doesn't play a whole game straight away. They travel across
a map of islands and learn one piece on each.

### The kingdom map

The islands follow one another along a winding path: **The Kingdom** (the
board as a town with streets and houses), **Little Bridesmaids** (pawns),
**Rook on Wheels**, **Butterfly** (bishop), **Queen**, **Pony** and
**Daddy King**. Each island has three to six lessons, and each lesson a few
short tasks. The next island opens once the previous one is done. Stars from
the lessons add up on the map, so the child can see how much they've already
mastered.

Seven islands and 31 lessons are finished so far, from "tap the room e4" to
"save the king". Six more (Battles and Apples, Checkmate, Magic Moves,
Openings, Tricks and The Big Game) are locked on the map, waiting to be
written.

### Lessons as a game

A lesson opens with a short story and then sets a task: drive the rook to the
star, collect all the stars, avoid the lava, take all the rival's pawns, tap
every square the pony can jump to, step the king out of check. When the child
taps a piece, the squares it may move to light up. Lessons without a king use
only piece moves, with no check and no castling. The real rules arrive step by
step.

Nobody stops the child. The number of moves only decides whether they get
three stars or two. When things aren't working, the app shows where the piece
may go after the first mistake, and suggests the first move after the third.
Every lesson is checked automatically: a solver walks through all the moves
and confirms the task can be done in the set number of moves.

### Everything is read aloud

The app works without reading. A narrator reads the story, the task, the
"well done" and "let's try again" replies and the characters' lines aloud.
Tapping the speech bubble reads the task again. The voices are pre-recorded
from text (Piper for Czech, Kokoro for English), so they sound the same on
every phone and work offline.

### Battles

When a piece captures, the board disappears for a moment and a duel plays out
full screen. The characters face each other in the rival's arena, swap a few
words ("Choo-choo!" – "Off to the pond…") and one of them goes off to rest.
Nobody dies: the defeated piece heads "back to the depot" or "off to the
meadow". A battle lasts under three seconds and can be skipped with a tap.

### Fourteen armies

It started with two armies: the **Fairies** (King Rainbeard, Princess Sparkle,
Daisy Tower, a unicorn instead of the pony) and the **Witches** (King Grumble,
who grumbles but loves cupcakes, Queen Thunder, whose spells go boom but never
hurt anyone, Batty Tower, and frogs in hats instead of pawns).

Now there are fourteen. Twelve vinyl-toy armies joined them: the **Kingdom**
(King Goldapple with his golden apple), the **Little Devils** (King
Grumblehorn with a big book and even bigger horns), **Cats**, **Dogs**,
**Bees**, **Hornets**, **Dinos** (King Rexy, big but kind), **Ice Age**,
**Fruit**, **Veggies**, and the toy **Woodland Fairies** and **Hedge
Witches**. Every army has six named characters, its own arena, greetings and
battle lines. The king is the tallest and the pawn the smallest, so the pieces
can be told apart on the board by height.

The child chooses who to play as and against whom. The characters are
original, with no brands or well-known fairy-tale licences. The fairies and
witches were made with FLUX.1-schnell, the toy armies with Qwen-Image. All of
them were then hand-picked and cleaned up.

### Hall of fame

Under the trophy on the map is the Hall of Fame with three shelves, all offline.
**The Golden Board** holds the 64 most famous games, from the Immortal Game
(1851) to Gukesh (2024), one on each square, and a game played to the end leaves
a star on its square. **Matches for the Crown** has every world championship
match since 1886 with the score and all the games, including the split FIDE
title of 1993–2006. **Women Champions** shows the women's world champions from
Vera Menchik to Hou Yifan with a selection of their games.

A game can be replayed in two ways. **Story** is for the child: the pieces of
their own army move by themselves, a speech bubble pops up at a check, a
castle or a bold sacrifice, and at most four big battles are picked from the
whole game. Every other capture just goes "poof". At the end comes checkmate
with a trophy, a resignation (the king takes a bow) or a draw (a handshake).
**Chronicle** is for the parent: the move list, step forward and back, and no
battles.

### Live tournaments

If you want, you can turn on "Games from the internet" in the hall of fame.
A big tournament that is being played right now then shows up there, first of
all the 2026 world championship match in Geneva. Finished rounds can be
replayed like the famous games, even offline. The switch is off by default.
The app then connects nowhere, and it downloads games only from its own small
server, with no account, cookies or tracking.

### Playing against the robot

The pawn war at the end of the bridesmaids' island is played against the
computer, against Baby the robot. The robot has four strength levels, from
completely clueless to one that already spots captures. A standalone game
against the robot, or against a parent on one phone, is still to come.

### Status

The first test version (1.0.4) is out: the Android APK can be downloaded below,
and iPhone and iPad get it through TestFlight. Next up are the remaining
islands, a parents' zone (settings, turning battles off, an overview of what the
child can do), stories and voice for the famous games, and a test with a real
five-year-old chess player: can she tell the fairies apart on a small board?

No ads, no account, works offline. The app is in 45 languages; the voice
speaks Czech and English so far.

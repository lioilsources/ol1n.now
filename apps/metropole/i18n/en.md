tagline: Free driving through the real New York, Prague and Tokyo on a portrait phone — with minigames from a vertical feed
---
Metropole is a game about driving a car through real cities. Streets, bridges,
buildings, parks, rivers and hills come from open map data, so Wenceslas
Square, Times Square and Ginza are where they belong. You play with one thumb
on a portrait phone, right in the browser, with no install and no account.

### How to drive

The car cruises on its own. Drag your thumb left and right to steer, down to
brake, up to boost; a second finger is the handbrake. The camera is either top
down like a map or behind the car, where you see the buildings, the hills and
the sky. Hitting a wall bounces the car off or lets it slide along. If it gets
stuck, it frees itself and goes back to the road.

### Three cities

**New York** – Manhattan up to 110th Street with four bridges, taxis and a flat
vector look with building shadows. **Prague** – the centre with trams on the
real tracks, pedestrian zones with bollards, the Vltava and the hills of Petřín,
Letná and Hradčany, drawn like a risograph print. **Tokyo** – left-hand traffic
and an anime cel look. You travel between the cities from the in-game phone.

### Feed, minigames and the Passport

The in-game phone has a vertical feed of cards like a social app: up for the
next one, down to go back, right for "let's go". Every card is a minigame at a
real place in the city, and the navigation takes you there. There are over
thirty of them, from checkers, Prší and gomoku to pool, bowling and minigolf,
and local games like Prague marbles, coin line and footvolley, or New York
stickball. Wins pay for cars and garage upgrades; landmarks you visit give
stamps and postcards in your Passport.

The city has traffic with traffic lights, pedestrians who always jump aside, and
police who chase and fine you. No weapons and no killing. Cars and shops are
made up; landmarks and public institutions keep their real names.

### Status

The game is in development and the web version is playable. A phone app exists
but is not released yet. The code is open on GitHub; the map data is under the
ODbL (© OpenStreetMap contributors, Overture Maps Foundation).

Metropole is a portrait game for one thumb. You drive around real cities —
**Prague, New York, Tokyo and Moscow** — built from real map data: streets,
bridges, embankments, hills and tram tracks are where they belong. Every city
has its own flat illustrated style. In between drives you play minigames that
the feed on your phone offers you, and earn money for more cars.

The web version plays right away in the browser (button above, best on a
phone); iOS and Android versions are on the way.

### Driving

Move your thumb left and right to steer, drag down to brake, up to boost, and
put a second finger down for the handbrake. The car can hop, too. The camera
is either top-down or an oblique chase view. Prague has hills (the altitude is
in the corner), and there are bridges, trams, pedestrians and traffic, day and
night, and weather — clear, rain and fog; the car grips less in the rain.

### Minigames from the feed

There are **36 minigames**: cards (Prší, Twenty-one, Hearts, Durak), board
games (draughts, five in a row, four in a row, mill, reversi, battleships),
dice, pool, minigolf, darts, bowling, a shooting gallery, air hockey, arcade
games and driving challenges — a drift zone, an A-to-B sprint and a taxi
shift. Some games belong to one city only: stickball and double dutch in New
York, marbles, coin line and footnet in Prague, gorodki, durak and boulevard
chess in Moscow.

You find games the way you would in real life:

- **The feed on your phone** — portrait cards you swipe through. Each shows
  the game, the place, the distance, the difficulty and what it pays; the Go
  button sets up navigation.
- **Ringing phone booths** — stop and pick up.
- **Spots in the city** — marked with the game's icon; stop there and play.
- **The Gallery** — every game in one list, when you don't feel like driving.

### Money

A finished minigame pays a base of **60** times the game type's factor (board
games 120 %, driving games 110 %, puzzles 90 %, everything else 100 %) times a
share by stars (50 / 80 / 100 % for one, two and three stars). If you drove
there from a feed card, a **trip bonus** is added: 50 + 20 for every minute of
the drive, again scaled by stars — at three stars exactly what the card
promised. A high score adds +10 / +25 / +50, and finishing a game for the
first time +100.

You spend it in the **Garage**: over forty fictional cars, tuning (engine,
tyres, brakes, weight, drift) and looks (paint, decals, wheels, underglow,
smoke), and every car can be test-driven first. Fares, towing a wrecked car
(75) and police fines cost money too. Your cash never drops below zero.

### Metro and taxi

You don't have to cross a big city by car every time. **Metro stations**
unlock when you visit them, and then you ride between unlocked stations for a
fare (30 + 4 per kilometre). The **taxi app** on your phone takes you to any
minigame or landmark for a distance-based price (60 + 25 per kilometre). Not
during a minigame, a taxi shift, or while the police are after you.

The **taxi shift**, on the other hand, is a minigame: four minutes behind the
wheel of a cab, with passengers naming real landmarks they want to go to. You
earn the meter, a bonus for a fast ride (or a deduction for a slow one) and a
tip that shrinks with every crash. Stars go by the takings of the whole shift.

### Passport, people and police

The **Passport** collects postcards — 25 or more landmarks in every city, each
with an illustration in the city's style — and district stamps. You meet
people around town who have a word with you, and some give you their number.
Crashes, driving on the pavement or leaving the scene of an accident bring
the **police**: there are three wanted levels, and at the third they set up
roadblocks. If they catch you, the fine is 100, 250 or 500.

### No real brands

Cars, shops and logos in the game are made up, no real brands. Maps come from
OpenStreetMap and the Overture Maps Foundation.

The app never tells the story for you. It hands you the building blocks —
characters, a quest, a problem, an ending — and then keeps the outline in
front of your eyes. You do the telling, to your own child, in your own words.

It all starts on the globe. Spin the planet, tap a country, and it decides
which tradition tonight’s tale comes from. The globe is also an honest
coverage map: green countries really do have tales in the corpus, grey ones
don’t yet. On a grey country the button is deliberately disabled — if it went
through, you’d get a tradition from somewhere else under that country’s label,
and that is the one thing the globe promises never to do.

Every country has its own landmark on the globe — the Eiffel Tower, Big Ben,
Prague Castle, the Taj Mahal, the pyramids — 241 stickers in all, so a child
recognises a country before being able to read its name. Add to that rivers,
mountain ranges, volcanoes, deserts and rainforests, drawn the way a
children’s atlas would draw them. Small countries would get lost on the whole
planet, so from afar Europe, the Caribbean or Southeast Asia are a single
piece; tap, and the globe flies in closer, where every country stands on its
own.

### From cast to outline

Pick one to six characters; each one can be re-rolled into another or deleted.
Then a quest for the hero, a problem that gets in the way, and an ending.
Together they make up the outline — a single screen you look at while you tell
the story.

The country you choose filters everything that follows: pick Denmark, and the
cast and all three sets of motifs show only Danish material. A narrow
tradition therefore offers **fewer** cards rather than topping itself up from
elsewhere.

### Motifs come from the corpus, not from thin air

Quests, problems and endings come from fairy tales in the public domain — the
Brothers Grimm, Andersen, Perrault, Aesop, Erben, Němcová, Lang’s Fairy Books
and collections from Africa, Asia, the Caucasus and Latin America, **2,795
tales from 98 countries** in all. Motifs are extracted from them, and a model
writes Czech versions of them for different ages. The pictures for characters
and motifs are real renders, not colour gradients sitting where the artwork
was supposed to be.

**The original, not a translation.** Where a tale has a Czech original (Erben,
Němcová), the Czech text is written from it — with the names and turns of
phrase of the original, not by translating back from English.

### Prompter

While you tell the story, the Prompter runs step by step — who is in it, where
they are, what went wrong, how it turns out. When you get stuck, you ask for a
hint. You don’t get a sentence to read out; you get a nudge.

The hints aren’t a handful of hand-written templates. Your phone turns
whatever is happening right now into a vector (you can dictate it, too) and
finds the closest matches in a pack of thousands of hints — close to the
motifs in your outline and to the stage of the story you’re at. It also shows
an illustration of the scene and, when you move on to the next step, suggests
how to pick up the thread. Before the telling, the outline is condensed into a
“tale in a nutshell”. The search model runs right on the phone, so none of
this needs a connection.

### Sounds and characters

Below the hints is a soundboard. A quietly playing melody matches the setting
of the step — forest, village, palace, sea — and turns tense when the problem
arrives. Next to it are buttons with the sounds of the animals and creatures
from your tale, and with spells and actions: wolf, owl, water sprite, dragon,
transformation, curse, gallop, thunder. Nothing plays by itself and nobody
speaks; you set the sound off when it fits your telling.

For Czechia you can choose from hundreds of fairy-tale characters, each with
its own illustration.

### Story packs

**Czechia is completely free** — all 127 Czech tales, each one illustrated.
From every other country, **five tales are free**. Europe comes with the app;
the other continents (Africa, Asia, the Americas, Oceania) are a free download
with a single tap on the globe; each pack is verified after download and can
be uninstalled when you run out of space.

The typeface is chubby and easy to read, the headings “bubbly” — the app is
for children, and it should look the part.

The whole flow Globe → Characters → Quest → Problem → Ending → Outline →
Prompter runs on the phone, without a single call to a server.

A child slides a finger across a keyboard of pictures and builds syllables and words — just the way they hear them. No timers, no penalties: a mistake only gives a little shake and a hint. Every unit finished earns a new animal for the Zoo.

Nine languages: Czech, English, German, Spanish, Italian, French, Portuguese, Chinese (pinyin) and Japanese (hiragana). The method follows how schools teach — Czech analytic-synthetic, English phonics, the others syllable by syllable. From syllables to words, on to listening without text, fill-in-the-gaps, rhymes and reviewing whatever is hardest; learned words go straight into a sentence and into My Book.

Menus, help, the parents' corner and the intro can be in the **family language** — any of 12: Ukrainian, Russian and Vietnamese join the nine above. So a child who speaks something else at home can learn to read in, say, Czech.

The game is hosted by **Pandy**. She stands right on the screen, can be nudged aside with a finger, cheers, reads, sleeps at night and waves when tapped. System emoji have been replaced by 294 illustrated stickers in her style, and the whole app is set in a chunky, child-friendly typeface.

In the Zoo the child plays with the animals: strokes them, tickles them and gives them gifts made of words they already know — food into the bowl, drinks into the water bowl. The animal eats and a sentence appears (“The mouse eats an apple.”) that can be saved to My Book. Each sibling picks their own guide: Pandy, Cappy, Raffie or Cheetie.

The map is a living world: every unit has its own habitat, the seasons follow the calendar, day turns into night, and you can hear the forest, the pond and the crickets. Stickers, secret stickers and badges — no streaks and no pressure.

For parents, behind a gate: which letter the child is struggling with, tips for home, a daily time limit, sibling profiles, a left-handed keyboard and a dyslexia-friendly font.

Works fully offline, collects no data, no ads and no purchases.

Built with Flutter.

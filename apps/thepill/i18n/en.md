The Pill is for parents of young kids and their partners. When you're both exhausted, one sharp message is all it takes to ruin the evening. The Pill doesn't forbid anything and never sends anything for you: it just offers a calmer version at the moment you're typing something that sounds like an attack.

### A coach in your keyboard

The Pill is a keyboard for Android and iPhone. You type as usual, and when you pause for a moment, the coach reads your message. If it sounds like blame, sarcasm, swearing or emotional blackmail, a bar appears above the keyboard: **"This sounds like an attack — show it differently?"** Tap it and you'll see a short explanation and two versions that say the same thing: the same time, the same request, the same feeling, just without the attack.

The keyboard inserts the version you pick only when you tap it. You always send it yourself, and if you ignore the bar, exactly what you wrote goes out.

### The facts stay

A rewrite must never lose anything the other person relies on. "WHERE THE HELL ARE THE KEYS" becomes "Asking where the keys are", and the daycare pickup time stays the same to the minute. When the coach isn't sure, it shows the original. A lost fact is a bug for us, and we test for it with every change.

### Privacy

The text of the message you're typing goes to The Pill server for a tone check. The server doesn't store or log it. The keyboard never sends passwords, numbers, phone numbers or e-mail addresses. When the server doesn't respond, the coach stays quiet and the keyboard keeps typing like any other. On iPhone the coach needs "Full Access"; without it, the keyboard isn't allowed on the network.

### What's next

On Android, softened incoming messages from people you mark yourself are up next: instead of a sharp notification you'll see a calm version of it, with the original one tap away. iPhone doesn't allow this, so we won't promise it there. After that comes a couple mode that you both switch on and either of you can switch off.

Still in development: the coach has passed its first tests on sample messages, and the keyboard is now heading to its first phones. Before it goes to the App Store and Google Play, we'll try it out with a few couples.

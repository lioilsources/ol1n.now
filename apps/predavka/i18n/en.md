tagline: A calm kitchen at rush hour — cooks hear the orders, servers see how many minutes until what's ready
---
At rush hour, a restaurant loses time between the kitchen and the floor. Tickets pile up on the line, servers walk into the kitchen to find out when table seven will be ready, and food that's done goes cold on the pass because nobody noticed it. Předávka fixes this without anyone staring at more screens: **cooks hear the orders**, **servers see on one display how many minutes until what arrives and for which table**, and finished food on the pass asks to be picked up on its own after a few minutes.

Everything runs in the restaurant on one small computer: no cloud, no monthly server fees, and in Czech. If the internet goes down, service carries on.

### The kitchen listens, no reading needed

When an order comes in, a speaker at the pass says a short sentence, such as “Table seven: two svíčková, one schnitzel.” Numbers and dish names follow correct Czech grammar (“jedna svíčková, dvě svíčkové, pět svíčkových”). The voice is generated right in the restaurant and in tests was ready to play within 0.2 s of the event. Announcements never talk over each other: they are ranked by importance, with a pause in between, and an old order that the display already shows isn't read out for nothing. The “Once more” button repeats the last announcement.

### A queue the cooks pull from

Each station (grill, hot, cold, desserts) has its own queue on a tablet or TV. A cook takes a dish with one tap or a press of the bump bar. That way the system knows who is cooking what and since when, and if nobody takes an order for two minutes, it speaks up: “Table seven, grill, waiting for a cook.”

### Servers know when and where to carry

The display on the floor shows the tables, what's going to them and in how many minutes. The estimate doesn't jump around (it changes at most once every half minute), so you can plan by it. Whatever needs picking up is always at the top. On the pass, food “ages”: after three minutes you hear “Table seven ready, three minutes on the pass”, and the announcement repeats until someone takes it.

### Estimates learn from your own kitchen

At the start, the head chef enters once how long each dish takes. From then on, Předávka times every dish from the moment a cook takes it until it's done, and after five measurements it uses this kitchen's real times. The estimate for a guest takes into account how much is in the queue ahead of them and how many cooks at the station are cooking at once.

### Works with any POS

Orders can be taken from the ticket printer: Předávka plugs in between the POS and the printer, the ticket prints as before, and it reads the table and dishes off it. So it needs no integration with any particular POS. Orders can also be entered straight from the servers' tablet.

### Dish pictures

A menu item can have a picture or an emoji. The browser displays show them on the order buttons, on the kitchen cards, on the pass and on the floor. The same system can also run at home as a kids' restaurant game: one kitchen, a kids' menu with pictures, a computer acting as both server and speaker, and the other devices just open a browser on the same Wi-Fi.

A detailed description of the features and of a whole service is on the [How it works](predavka-features.html) page (in Czech), including three moments of an evening: an empty queue, a few tables and “we're swamped!”.

### Status

Předávka is a finished working prototype: the server, voice, printer listening, queue, estimates, courses and displays (an app for Android TV and tablets plus a browser version) are built and tested in a simulated service. **It hasn't run in a real restaurant yet.** The next step is a pilot in one kitchen: how clear the voice is over the noise of the extractor hood, covering the kitchen with several speakers, and how readable the displays are on real TVs.

The screenshots come from real displays. What they show was produced by simulating a service: a made-up menu and tables, with time frozen at a given moment of the evening.
